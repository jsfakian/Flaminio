# Writes, RF 1, with zero-copy writes: short validation (2026-10-19)

Comparison A of the [performance plan](../performance-plan.md) after W4.3, with the method of
the [W4.2 campaign](2026-10-19-writes-rf1-w42.md):
- fl-b (NVMe, 4 vCPUs), zero copy, one ublk queue;
- O_DSYNC on both sides;
- every run from a reset disk, written whole, preconditioned, then idle 5 minutes;
- judged at steady state.

**This is a short validation, not a campaign:** one repetition of each job, not 3. A single
repetition has no spread, so differences of a few percent below are not findings. The question
was whether the change cost throughput, and whether it still passes the integrity checks.

Build: commit `a64ef64`.

## What changed since W4.2

- **One copy of a write's data in user space, down from four.** Before, the data was copied
  from the ublk staging slot:
  - into a `Vec`;
  - into each chunk's piece;
  - into each replica's frame;
  - into an aligned buffer for O_DIRECT.
- **Now it is copied once,** from the staging slot into an aligned buffer, shared as
  `bytes::Bytes` and passed by reference:
  - the engine slices it per chunk;
  - every replica's frame shares it, and a received frame wraps the receive buffer;
  - the store hands slices of it to the device as its redirect-on-write data, and the journal
    batch is shared with the device too;
  - the device writes an aligned buffer as it is, holding a reference until the write
    completes. Any other buffer is copied into an aligned one, as before.
- **Nothing else moves.** Commits, gathering, staging and the order in which batches become
  durable are W4.2's. The change is in how the bytes travel, not when.
- **What it should give:** less CPU per unit of work. Writes here wait on the flush, not on
  the CPU, so throughput should stay where it was.

## Results

Steady state, one repetition:

| Job | Raw | W4.2 | **W4.3** | Share of raw |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4K random, QD32 | unsteady | 9 847 IOPS | **10 512** | see below |
| 70/30 random, 4K QD32 (reads + writes) | 23 634 IOPS | 26 317 | **27 112** | 114% |
| 1M sequential, QD8 | 1 653 MiB/s | 701 | **697** | 42% (see below) |

(W4.2's figures are its campaign's medians of 3. W4.1 had 10 236, 26 534 and 541.)

- **Throughput is flat.** 1M is 697 MiB/s against 701. 4K QD32 and the mixed job are 7% and
  3% above W4.2's medians, which one repetition cannot tell from noise: W4.2's own spread on
  4K QD32 was 4.1%.
- **4K QD32 has no share:** raw's repetition did not reach steady state, so there is no raw
  figure to divide by. In W4.2's campaign raw gave 7 927 IOPS, and Flaminio was at 124%.
- **Write amplification,** as with W4.2:

| Job | Disk, Flaminio | Drive, Flaminio |
|---|---:|---:|
| 4K random, QD32 | 2.22 | 1.00 |
| 70/30 random, 4K QD32 | 2.25 | 1.00 |
| 1M sequential, QD8 | 1.00 | 1.00 |

  Raw is 1.00 on both counts. The journal doubles small writes on the disk, and the drive
  adds nothing on top.

## CPU

Measured on fl-b when the change was made, as the ublk queue thread's share of a core per
MiB/s of writes:
- **1M sequential:** 0.049% → 0.044%, about **10% less**.
- **4K QD32:** about 10% less, too.

The runs above do not include a profile: this table is the commit's measurement, not this
validation's.

## 1M sequential

- **Against the raw measured here:** raw's figure comes from rewriting one 16 GiB span, which
  the drive keeps in its fast cache: 1 653 MiB/s in this run, so Flaminio's 697 is **42%**.
  The W4.2 campaign's raw ranged 1 655–1 967 MiB/s.
- **The fair reference,** as in W4.2: raw written across the disk, as Flaminio's store writes,
  sustains about **1 000 MiB/s** ([measured on 2026-10-17](2026-10-17-writes-rf1-w41.md)).
  Against that, Flaminio is at about **70%**, as with W4.2.
- **What remains:** the flush, one commit at a time. Raw keeps 8 writes in flight, each
  overlapping the others' flushes. W4.3 does not touch this, and it was not expected to.

## RF 2, 4K QD1

One repetition, same build, the volume as in [comparison B](2026-10-17-writes-rf2-w41.md)
(target on fl-b, replicas on fl-b and fl-a or fl-c):

| Job | Raw | W4.1 | **W4.3** | Share of raw |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4K random, QD1, RF 2 | 469 IOPS | 386 | **386** | 82% |

- **Same as W4.1's RF 2** (386 IOPS; 83% of that campaign's raw, 462).
- **QD1 only,** as a check that the shared buffer works across replicas. At 1M, RF 2 is bound
  by the 1 GbE link.
- **Write amplification:** Flaminio 3.34 on the disk and 1.03 on the drive. Raw's drive figure
  was 1.62 in this run.
- **The network,** measured before and after, over the WireGuard overlay the replicas use:
  112 MB/s each way to fl-a and fl-c; round trip 1.96 → 1.82 ms to fl-a, 1.97 → 1.66 ms to
  fl-c.

## Integrity

After every Flaminio run:
1. the volume was written with a checksum in every block and read back;
2. the node restarted, replaying its journal, on the verify path;
3. the random writes were checked again.

**RF 1: 3 runs, 9 checks: 0 errors, 0 Flaminio checksum failures. RF 2: 1 run, 3 checks: 0 errors,
0 Flaminio checksum failures.**

W4.3 changes no durability rule: only how the data is held on its way to the device.

## What this does not show

- **No spread:** one repetition per job. The full 3-repetition campaign has not been run for
  W4.3.
- **RF 2 at QD32, 70/30 and 1M** were not run. Frames now share the write's buffer on every
  replica, and that has only been exercised at QD1 here.
- **No new profile:** the CPU figures are the commit's.
