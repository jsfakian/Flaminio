# Writes, RF 1 against the raw disk (2026-10-14)

`make bench-writes` on fl-b (NVMe, 4 vCPUs), comparison A of the
[performance plan](../performance-plan.md) (W3): a volume with a single replica, on the node's
own disk, against the same disk raw. Zero copy, one ublk queue, no polling.

## Method

Every run, on each side, starts from the same state (W1, option 3):
1. **Reset:**
   - stop all I/O;
   - discard the guest's data disk (QEMU punches holes in the image);
   - trim the host's filesystem and preallocate the image again;
   - idle 5 minutes, then drop caches in guest and host.
2. **Fresh round:** one minute of the job.
3. **Whole disk written once,** sequentially. Flaminio's store is then formatted again and the
   volume created again.
4. **Precondition:** 16 GiB span, sequential 128 KiB writes, twice over.
5. **Settle:** idle 5 minutes.
6. **Steady rounds:** 1-minute rounds until 5 in a row stay within ±10% of their mean, with no
   trend (at most 12).
7. **Integrity** (Flaminio only): see below.

Both sides use O_DSYNC: Flaminio acknowledges a write once it is durable, and the raw disk's
writes are durable only with it. 3 repetitions, interleaved (raw, Flaminio, raw, ...).

Steps 3 and 5 were added during this work, each after a measurement showed the comparison was
unfair without it:
- **The host's first writes are slow.** The lab's disks are files, and after the reset the
  host's first write to each block costs 3–4× more: its filesystem converts the preallocated,
  unwritten extents (1M at QD1: 145 against 460 MB/s).
  - The raw side rewrites one span.
  - The store allocates over the whole disk with a rotating cursor, so its steady state kept
    paying for first writes: 1M writes at 7% of raw.
- **The drive needs idle time to catch up.** After the large writes of steps 3 and 4, it stays
  in a slow mode while under load, for more than 20 minutes (4K QD1: 275 against 460 IOPS).
  It works off that debt only when idle; 5 minutes are enough.
  - Without the wait, runs switched modes at random, mid-run, on both sides.

6 187. One raw QD32 repetition did not settle in 12 rounds (5 767–9 413 IOPS); the other two
gave 8 823 and 8 929.

|---|---:|---:|

**Fresh state** (the first minute after the reset): Flaminio matches raw at QD1 (143 against 148

## Integrity

After every Flaminio run:
1. fio wrote the whole volume (1M sequential) and then 2 GiB of 4K random writes over it, with
   a checksum in every block, and read each back.
2. The node restarted, which replays the store's journal, on the verify path (every read also
   checked against the store's checksums).
3. fio checked the random writes again.

**12 runs, 36 checks: 0 errors, and 0 Flaminio checksum failures.** Local reads now log every
checksum mismatch, also the ones a retry recovers, so the count cannot miss one.
Data: [`data/2026-10-14-writes-rf1/`](data/2026-10-14-writes-rf1/).

## What changed to get here

The first runs found two flaws in how ublk writes reach the store:
- **Every write committed alone** (148 IOPS at 4K QD32, fresh).
  - A tag task pumped the engine right after submitting, so the store's group commit saw one
    write at a time, with a flush each.
  - Now the queue thread pumps once per event loop round, after running its tasks.
- **Zero copy split the groups:** 4 239–4 424 IOPS against 6 638 on the copy path.
  - With zero copy, a write's data reaches the staging area through an io_uring copy. These
    copies completed over several rounds, so the writes reached the store in small groups.
  - Now the pump waits while writes are being staged: 11 056–11 228 IOPS.

## Where writes stand

- **QD1 and QD32 meet the plan's bound** (≥ 80% of raw O_DSYNC).
  - At QD32, Flaminio beats raw: raw pays one flush per write; a group commit pays one for up
    to 32 writes, each acknowledged only once durable.
- **1M sequential and the mixed job do not.** Both have the same cause: the store commits on
  the ublk queue thread, synchronously:
  1. the data and the journal batch, written together;
  2. one flush (recovery checks the last batch's data against the checksums it carries);
  3. small writes applied in place.

  While it does:
  - the next group cannot start (1M: 8 MiB per commit, about 13 ms each);
  - reads wait behind the flush (the mixed job: raw keeps reading while its writes flush);
  - the other queues wait too, as the commit holds the engine's lock.

**Next (W4):** commits off the queue thread: the commit's writes and flush run on the ring,
like reads, so reads are served meanwhile and the next group gathers. Then pipelined: the next
group's writes overlap the previous group's flush.
