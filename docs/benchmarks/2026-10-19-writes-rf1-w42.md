# Writes, RF 1, with overlapped commits (2026-10-19)

Comparison A of the [performance plan](../performance-plan.md) after W4.2, with the method of
the [W4.1 campaign](2026-10-17-writes-rf1-w41.md):
- fl-b (NVMe, 4 vCPUs), zero copy, one ublk queue;
- O_DSYNC on both sides;
- every run from a reset disk, written whole, preconditioned, then idle 5 minutes;
- 3 interleaved repetitions, judged at steady state.

Build: commit `0f6bd66`.

## What changed since W4.1

- **Overlapped commits.** While a commit is in flight, the writes that arrive behind it are
  staged:
  - their redirect-on-write data goes to the disk at once, during the current flush;
  - their journal batch is written as soon as the current commit lands;
  - its flush also covers that early data.
- **Recovery's rules are unchanged.** Batches still become durable one at a time and in order,
  and early data goes to clusters that no durable batch references until its own batch does.
- **Only writes that cover a whole cluster are staged.** A small write into a mapped cluster
  is all journal, so it has nothing to send early. Staging it would bypass gathering: that
  took 4K QD32 down to 6 700 IOPS in a first campaign, stopped after 10 runs.
- **The device's flush:** the device sequences a commit's flush itself, once the commit's own
  writes have completed. An `IOSQE_IO_DRAIN` flush would also hold back the early writes queued
  after it.

## 1M sequential

- **Gain:** 701 MiB/s, up 30% from 541 with W4.1. The median latency drops from 13.4 to
  10.4 ms.
- **The fair reference:** raw's figure comes from rewriting one 16 GiB span, which the drive
  keeps in its fast cache. Written across the disk, as Flaminio's store writes, raw sustains
  about **1 000 MiB/s** ([measured on 2026-10-17](2026-10-17-writes-rf1-w41.md)). Against
  that, Flaminio is at about **70%**, up from 53%.
- **Over 10 minutes of 1M:** 590–720 MiB/s sustained, against 500–560 with W4.1. The first
  40 s, still in the drive's cache, gave 888–1 412 MiB/s.
- **What remains:**
  - Each commit carries about 4 MiB, half of what the client keeps in flight. The pipeline
    has two stages: half the writes flushing, the other half writing their data.
  - What limits it now is the flush itself, one at a time. Raw keeps 8 writes in flight, each
    overlapping the others' flushes.

## Integrity

After every Flaminio run:
1. the volume was written with a checksum in every block and read back;
2. the node restarted, replaying its journal, on the verify path;
3. the random writes were checked again.

**12 runs, 36 checks: 0 errors, 0 Flaminio checksum failures.**

The store's tests also cover W4.2's failure cases:
- a power cut at 4 points of a staging pipeline × 8 random crash images keeps exactly the
  writes whose commits landed;
- a staged group that never commits leaves no leaked clusters;
- a pipeline that fills the journal stops staging and goes on.
