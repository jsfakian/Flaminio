# Writes, RF 2 against RF 1 and the raw disk (2026-10-17)

Comparison B of the [performance plan](../performance-plan.md) (W3): the cost of replication.
- **Method and build:** the RF 1 campaign's, the same day
  ([results](2026-10-17-writes-rf1-w41.md)). fl-b, zero copy, one ublk queue, O_DSYNC on both
  sides, every run from a reset disk, 3 interleaved repetitions, steady state. Build
  `caa15b0`, with commits off the queue thread and gathering.
- **The volume:** RF 2, target on fl-b. Every chunk has a replica on fl-b and one on fl-a or
  fl-c (32 chunks each), all in the ISR.
- **Not reset:** only fl-b's disk is reset before each run. fl-a's and fl-c's hold vol1 and
  cannot be. They are files on rhythm, the other server.

**The network,** measured in the same campaign over the WireGuard overlay the replicas use:

| | fl-b → fl-a | fl-b → fl-c |
|---|---|---|
| TCP throughput (iperf3, 10 s each way), start and end | 112 MB/s out, 112 MB/s in | 112 MB/s out, 112 MB/s in |
| Round trip, start → end | 1.96 → 1.80 ms | 1.55 → 1.65 ms |

## What replication costs

- **Small writes: 5–8% against RF 1.**
  - The target sends each write to the peer while it commits locally, so a write waits for
    the slower of the two, not both.
  - The peers are fast: during the 4K QD32 rounds each wrote about 40 MiB/s with about 7 000
    writes and 1 000 flushes per second. Their round trip plus their commit is close to fl-b's
    local commit (2.3 ms at QD1).
  - That matches the plan's bound for latency: RF 1 plus about one round trip at most.
- **1M sequential: the network.** 106 MiB/s is the 1 GbE link: 112 MB/s, or 106.8 MiB/s. Every
  byte goes once to the peer. That is the plan's bound for bandwidth, and comparison C (RF 2
  against raw) needs a faster interconnect to mean anything (phase 6).
- **Reads are not replicated:** the mixed job's reads are served by the local replica, and it
  stays above raw (7 575 writes and 17 674 reads per second, against raw's 7 333 and 17 107).

## Integrity

After every Flaminio run, the same check as in the RF 1 campaign: the volume written with a
checksum in every block and read back, then reread after a node restart, on the verify path.
**12 runs, 36 checks: 0 errors, 0 Flaminio checksum failures.**
