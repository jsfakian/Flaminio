# Writes, RF 1 against the raw disk, with commits off the queue thread (2026-10-17)

Comparison A of the [performance plan](../performance-plan.md) again, after W4.1. The method
is the [first campaign's](2026-10-14-writes-rf1.md):
- fl-b (NVMe, 4 vCPUs), zero copy, one ublk queue;
- O_DSYNC on both sides;
- every run from a reset disk, written whole, then preconditioned and left idle;
- 3 interleaved repetitions, judged at steady state.

Build: commit `caa15b0`.

## What changed since the first campaign

- **Commits off the queue thread** (W4.1). The store's commit (the data and the journal
  batch, then one flush) runs on a second io_uring ring. Meanwhile the queue thread serves
  reads and gathers the next group. Nothing is applied before the batch is durable, and one
  commit is in flight at a time, so recovery's rules are unchanged.
- **Gathering.** After a commit, the next waits up to 300 µs for the writes it acknowledged
  to come back, so that a client keeping its queue full commits in full groups.
  - Without gathering, 32 writes in flight split into two halves that committed in turn.
  - 100 µs was too short in these VMs: 80% of the waits ran out.

**The mixed job:** Flaminio does 8 008 writes and 18 682 reads per second, against raw's
7 051 and 16 450. Reads no longer wait behind commits: before W4.1 the job was at 36%.

**Write amplification** is unchanged: 2.2–3.4 bytes to the disk per client byte for small
writes, which go through the journal; 1.00 for 1M writes.

## 1M sequential: two raw references

Raw's 1M figure comes from rewriting one 16 GiB span. The drive keeps that span in its fast
cache. Raw 1M QD8 with O_DSYNC, 3 minutes per pattern, MiB/s per 10 s:

| Pattern | First 30 s | Last 30 s |
|---|---:|---:|
| One 16 GiB span | 1 791–1 872 | 1 123–1 259 |
| Across the other 84 GiB | 935–1 036 | 1 007–1 029 |
| The span again, right after | 1 077–1 196 | 1 044–1 045 |

- The drive sustains about **1 000 MiB/s** of synchronous 1M writes once outside its cache.
  The campaign's raw rounds (5 minutes per run) stay mostly in the cache.
- Flaminio's store places new data across the whole disk, so it is soon outside the cache.
  Against raw written across the disk, Flaminio's 541 MiB/s is about **53%**.
- The allocation policy is not the cause: first-fit allocation, which reuses freed clusters
  and keeps data packed, gave the same 500–560 MiB/s over 10 minutes.

**What limits Flaminio at 1M:**
- Its commits run strictly one after the other: write about 4 MiB, flush, then the next.
  Raw keeps 8 writes in flight, each overlapping the others' flushes.
- About 130 flushes per second at 530 MiB/s means each commit carries about 4 MiB: half of
  the 8 MiB in flight.
- A 1M write takes long to come back after its acknowledgement: each 512 KiB piece is copied
  twice before it is queued. So gathering waits run out (75% of them), and the writes keep
  splitting into two halves.

This is W4.2 in the plan: the next group's data written during the current flush.

## Integrity

After every Flaminio run:
1. fio wrote the volume with a checksum in every block, 1M sequential and then 2 GiB of 4K
   random writes, and read each back.
2. The node restarted, replaying its journal, on the verify path.
3. fio checked the random writes again.

**12 runs, 36 checks: 0 errors, 0 Flaminio checksum failures.**

One check was cut off by a lost connection. The node's log showed no checksum error, and the
run was done again and passed. The harness had reported it as a failure; it now tells the
two apart.
