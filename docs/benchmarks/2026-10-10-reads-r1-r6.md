# Reads, steps R1 to R6 of the performance plan (2026-10-10)

`make bench-reads` on fl-b (NVMe, 4 vCPUs), by the rules of the
[performance plan](../performance-plan.md), against the
[zero-copy baseline](2026-10-09-zero-copy-reads.md) of the day before:
- `vol1`: 16 GiB, RF 2, a replica of every chunk on fl-b;
- raw jobs on 16 GiB spans of the same disk, at offsets 0, 32 and 64 GiB in turn;
- caches dropped (guest and host) before every job;
- 3 interleaved repetitions, 30 s per job.

Every job's spread is at most 4.4%. Every run ended with fio rereading the whole volume and
checking the checksum in every block: **0 errors in every run**. Every verify-mode run also
logged **0 Flaminio checksum failures**. The summaries are in
[`data/2026-10-10-r1-r5/`](data/2026-10-10-r1-r5/).

**R1:** the copy path without verification (an experiment, one repetition before the network
dropped) gave 191 832 IOPS at QD32 and 5 453 MiB/s at 1M. Verification then cost 40% of
1M's throughput, and the copy 18%.

## What each step did

- **R1, the experiments:** measured separately what verification and the copy cost. The copy
  matters at QD32, verification at 1M.
- **R2, polling** (`--poll-us`, off by default): QD1 from 84% to 88%. The queue thread spins
  instead of sleeping, which saves the wake-up of a halted vCPU, twice per read. It costs a
  whole core under load, so it is a setting for latency-sensitive volumes, not the default.
  - The remaining 9 µs per read (71 µs against raw's 62) are the ublk round trip itself:
    request to the queue thread, then commit.
- **R3, fast reads:** a read served by the local replica no longer goes through the target's
  queue, its messages and the engine's maps. The target admits it at once, by the same rules,
  and the frontend reads the store itself (protocol spec §5, fast reads).
  - QD32 rose from 88% to 95%, and the queue thread is no longer saturated (68%).
  - The simulator found two flaws in the first version, and a third that was already in the
    data plane's direct local reads. All three are fixed and covered by mutations; 320 000
    seeds pass.
- **R4, several queues** (`--queues N`): with two submitting threads, 2 queues take QD32 from
  90% to 94% and spread the work (busiest thread 80% → 62%). A single fio job maps to one queue
  whatever the count, as the kernel maps each submitting CPU to one queue, so the single-job
  numbers do not change. The engine is shared by the queue threads under a lock, held only
  between awaits.
  - *Note (2026-10-29, [issue #9](../9/design.md)):* the single-job numbers were measured with
    one queue only; there was no 2-queue run with one job. With one queue per vCPU (4 on
    fl-b), each queue's thread is pinned to the submitting CPU: QD1 gains about 2 µs per read,
    and one job at QD32 loses 11–12%, as fio and the thread share a CPU
    ([results](2026-10-29-reads-qd1-queues.md)).
- **R5, CRC-32C** (`crc-fast`): per 4 KiB block, 3.7× faster on fl-b's Xeon E-2436 (43 GB/s
  against 11.8) and 8.6× on fl-a's Xeon 6527P (84 GB/s against 9.8), on cache-hot data. On the
  verify path, 1M rose from 49% to 58%.
  - It stays below raw because the data arrives cold, by DMA. The checksum and the kernel's
    copy each read it from memory (`perf`: copy about 45% of the queue thread, CRC about 30%).
  - The verify path is the integrity run's path, as decided; zero copy is the performance
    path.
- **R6, zero copy:** done the day before, and decided: zero copy for performance, the verify
  path for integrity runs.

## Where reads stand

| | 4K random, QD1 | 4K random, QD32 | 1M sequential, QD8 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Plan's goal | ≥ 90% | ≥ 90% | ≥ 90% |
| Zero copy, default settings | 84% | **95%** | **≈ 100%** |
| Zero copy, 100 µs polling | 88% | 96% | 102% |

QD32 and 1M meet the goal. QD1 does not: even with polling it is 2 points short. The rest is
the ublk round trip, two context switches per read. A larger change would be needed: for
example, ublk's batched command interface (in libublk), or letting the kernel complete simple
reads without the round trip, as proposed for ublk with BPF (to be checked against what the
kernel offers).
